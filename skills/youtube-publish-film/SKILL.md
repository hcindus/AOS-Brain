# YouTube Publish & Discoverability

**KPI:** Get the film + Short surfaced in search and pushed by the algorithm, even on a 0-subscriber channel.

## Two uploads, two audiences
1. **Full film** → standard YouTube video.
2. **9:16 vertical trailer** → separate upload as a **YouTube Short** (reaches a completely different audience; Shorts get dramatically more reach for new channels).

## Settings that determine discoverability

### Title — be specific and searchable
- ✅ `AI Cinematic Short Film | Made with 7ART + Claude in 6 Days` (surfaces in search)
- ❌ `My AI Film` (invisible)

### Description — lead with WHAT, not HOW
- The first two lines show before the "read more" cut-off. Lead with what the video IS, not how it was made.
- Put tool credits + the YouTube Short link below the fold.

### Thumbnail — always custom
- A strong thumbnail drives more clicks than the title. **Never** use YouTube's auto-generated frame.

### Tags — mix specific and broad
- Specific: `7ART`, your genre (thriller, drama, sci-fi, etc.)
- Broad: `AI film`, `AI filmmaking`, `cinematic AI video`

### Quality — upload the highest you have
- Start at 4K; YouTube compresses everything, so 4K → better final quality after compression.

## Why upload on a 0-subscriber channel
Shorts don't care about subscriber count — they're distributed on **watch-through rate**. A ~20s cinematic trailer watched to the end gets pushed regardless of channel size. That's the entry point for a new channel.

## Rules
- Short = separate upload, never the 16:9 squeezed down.
- Always custom thumbnail; never auto-frame.
- Lead description with the content, not the tooling.
