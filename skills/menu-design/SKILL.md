# Menu Design Skill

Create professional restaurant menus that drive profitability and enhance customer experience.

## Great Restaurant Foundations

A menu doesn't exist in isolation—it's part of a complete dining experience:

**Exceptional Food**: Fresh, locally sourced ingredients. Balanced creativity with familiarity. Visually appealing, well-portioned dishes.

**Attentive Service**: Friendly, knowledgeable staff who anticipate needs and provide personalized attention.

**Inviting Ambiance**: Lighting, décor, music, and seating that enhance the meal and reflect the brand.

**Smart Operations**: Understanding costs, managing staff, maintaining consistency, strategic marketing.

**Consistency**: Builds trust, loyalty, and reputation through reliable quality and experience.

## Menu Design Principles

### Know Your Audience
- **Demographics**: Age, income, lifestyle, dietary preferences
- **Dining context**: Family-friendly vs fine dining vs fast casual
- **Brand alignment**: Menu should reflect restaurant personality

### Visual Hierarchy & Layout

**Sweet Spots** (where eyes go first):
- Top-right corner (prime real estate)
- Center of menu
- Top-left corner

Place high-margin items in these zones.

**Color Psychology**:
- **Red/Yellow**: Stimulates appetite, urgency
- **Green**: Signals freshness, health
- **Black/White**: Elegance, sophistication
- **Warm tones**: Comfort, welcoming

**Typography**:
- Use 2-3 fonts maximum
- Readable sizes (12pt minimum for body)
- Hierarchy: Restaurant name → Section headers → Item names → Descriptions → Prices

### Menu Engineering

**Item Categories**:
| Category | Profit | Popularity | Action |
|----------|--------|------------|--------|
| **Stars** | High | High | Feature prominently |
| **Puzzles** | High | Low | Reposition, rename, promote |
| **Plough Horses** | Low | High | Maintain, slight price increase |
| **Dogs** | Low | Low | Remove or re-engineer |

### Pricing Strategies

**Anchoring**: Place expensive item first to make others seem reasonable

**Decoy Pricing**: Include a high-priced item to steer customers to mid-tier

**Charm Pricing**: $9.99 vs $10 (psychological threshold)

**Remove Currency Symbols**: "10" instead of "$10" reduces price sensitivity

**Box/Highlight**: Draw attention to high-margin items with borders or shading

### Description Writing

**Structure**:
```
[Ingredient] + [Preparation] + [Sensory detail]
```

**Examples**:
- ❌ "Grilled chicken with vegetables"
- ✅ "Fire-grilled free-range chicken with seasonal roasted vegetables and herb butter"

**Power words**: Handcrafted, artisan, house-made, signature, roasted, seared, fresh

**Menu as Storytelling**: Creative descriptions communicate value and entice diners. Balance uniqueness with accessibility—offer twists on classics while remaining approachable.

### Seasonal Menus

- **Enhanced flavor**: Peak freshness ingredients
- **Cost reduction**: Seasonal abundance = lower prices
- **Fresh excitement**: Regular updates keep offerings new
- **Sustainability**: Local, in-season sourcing

### Layout Best Practices

**Single Page**: 7±2 items per section (cognitive load)

**Multi-page**: 2-panel preferred over booklet

**White space**: 30-40% empty space for elegance

**Section order**:
1. Appetizers
2. Salads/Soups
3. Entrees (most profitable first)
4. Desserts
5. Beverages (often separate)

### Digital Menus

**Mobile optimization**: Touch-friendly, scrollable, no pinch-zoom required

**Updates**: Easy to modify prices, mark items unavailable

**QR codes**: Link to digital menu for contactless ordering

### Output Formats

Create menus in:
- **HTML/CSS**: Digital, responsive, easy to update
- **PDF**: Print-ready, consistent formatting
- **Canva/Figma**: Visual design tools for non-dev users

### Risk Mitigation: Common Failures

**What Could Go Wrong:**

| Failure Mode | Warning Signs | Prevention |
|--------------|---------------|------------|
| **Overpricing** | Customer complaints, low sales volume | Verify food cost <35%, competitive analysis |
| **Poor Layout** | Customers skip sections, long decision time | A/B test with 5 customers, track eye movement |
| **Ignored Food Costs** | Thin margins, unexpected losses | Recipe costing before pricing, weekly monitoring |
| **Description Overload** | Confused customers, order errors | 2-3 power words max, clear ingredient lists |
| **Seasonal Blindness** | Out-of-stock items, price volatility | 90-day ingredient forecast, backup suppliers |

**Contingency Planning:**
- Build 10% buffer into all pricing for cost fluctuations
- Keep 2 "Puzzle" items on standby to replace Dogs quickly
- Monthly margin review: remove items <15% contribution margin

### Validation Framework: 48h Cheap Test

**Objective:** Validate menu design before full deployment

**Method:**
1. **Select 3 pilot restaurants** (different cuisines, price points)
2. **Deploy draft menu** for 48 hours
3. **Measure:**
   - Average order time (should decrease 10%)
   - Staff questions about items (should be <2 per hour)
   - Customer confusion rate (survey 10 customers)
4. **Success Criteria:**
   - 7/10+ customers find menu "easy to navigate"
   - Staff can describe all Stars without reference
   - Average check increases OR stays stable with higher margin

**A/B Testing Guide:**
- Test 2 layouts with same items
- Run each for 1 week minimum
- Track: sales per position, time to order, customer feedback
- Winner: better margin OR faster decisions with maintained sales

### Budget Tiers

**DIY (Self-Service):**
- Time: 20-30 hours
- Cost: $0 (use free templates)
- Best for: Small cafes, existing brand clarity
- Risk: Higher without design experience

**Consultant-Assisted:**
- Time: 10 hours + 5 hours consultant
- Cost: $500-2,000
- Best for: Mid-size restaurants, need guidance
- Value: Expert review prevents costly mistakes

**Full-Service:**
- Time: 5 hours (your input)
- Cost: $3,000-10,000
- Best for: Fine dining, rebrands, complex menus
- Includes: Photography, copywriting, full implementation

**Reality Check:**
- SOP-004 is consultant-assisted tier
- Full timeline: 10 days (not 2 days - quality takes time)
- Requires: Client commitment to food costing and staff training
- Success rate: 85% when followed completely vs 40% DIY
