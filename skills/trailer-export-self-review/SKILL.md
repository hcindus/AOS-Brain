# Trailer Export & Self-Assessment

**KPI:** Ship a trailer that survives the silent-scroll test, lands cuts on the beat, and leaves the viewer wanting the full film.

## Process

### 1. Export BOTH versions FIRST (before watching anything back)
Watching the raw timeline preview tricks your brain — you must review the actual exported file, the way an audience sees it.

| Version | Settings | Filename |
|---------|----------|----------|
| 16:9 master | 1080p or 4K, MP4, 24fps | `trailer_16x9.mp4` |
| 9:16 vertical | same settings | `trailer_9x16.mp4` |

Both go in `[Project] / FINAL /`.

### 2. Watch three times, one focus each

- **Watch 1 — The scroll test.** Imagine autoplaying in a feed with no sound. Does the FIRST shot make you stop? If not, replace it with your most visually striking frame.
- **Watch 2 — The beat test.** Close your eyes and just listen; open them on each beat. The shot change should land on the audio accent. If offset, shift clips a few frames.
- **Watch 3 — The "want more" test.** After it ends, do you want the full film? If the trailer gives away too much, remove the most revealing shot.

### 3. Fix + re-export
Fix the top three issues, re-export, move on.

### 4. Save deliverables
- Thumbnail screenshot (most striking frame)
- CapCut project file

## Length guidance
- Portfolio trailer: ~28s is fine.
- TikTok / Reels: under 20s is better — trim 2–3 middle shots by ~0.5s each for a tighter feel.

## Rules
- Always export before you review — never judge from the timeline preview.
- One focus per watch pass; don't try to catch everything at once.
- If it "tells the story," cut the most revealing shot.
