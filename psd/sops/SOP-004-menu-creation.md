# SOP-004: Menu Design & Creation

## Purpose
Standardize the process for creating professional, profitable restaurant menus that enhance customer experience and drive revenue.

## Scope
Applies to all menu creation and redesign projects for restaurant, café, bar, and food service clients.

## Prerequisites
- Client consultation completed
- Brand guidelines established
- Menu category determined (dine-in, takeout, digital, etc.)
- Pricing strategy approved

---

## Phase 1: Discovery & Strategy (Day 1)

### 1.1 Client Intake
| Task | Details | Output |
|------|---------|--------|
| Audience Analysis | Document target demographics, dietary preferences, price sensitivity | Customer Profile |
| Brand Alignment | Define restaurant personality, values, positioning | Brand Brief |
| Competitive Review | Analyze 3-5 competitor menus for positioning | Competitive Analysis |
| Concept Definition | Establish cuisine type, service style, atmosphere | Concept Document |

### 1.2 Food Cost Analysis
**Calculate true profitability before pricing:**

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Food Cost %** | (Ingredient Cost ÷ Menu Price) × 100 | 25-35% |
| **Contribution Margin** | Menu Price − Ingredient Cost | Maximum |
| **Plate Cost** | Sum of all ingredients + waste % | Track weekly |

**Required for each menu item:**
- [ ] Recipe costing sheet (all ingredients, weights, costs)
- [ ] Vendor pricing (update monthly)
- [ ] Waste/shrink factor (5-10% typical)
- [ ] Labor cost allocation (if applicable)
- [ ] Target margin (65-75% gross margin typical)

**Red Flags:**
- Food cost > 40% (unless premium positioning)
- Low-margin items with high labor complexity
- Ingredients with volatile pricing

**Deliverable:** Recipe Costing Worksheet

### 1.3 Menu Engineering
**Categorize all menu items by profitability AND popularity:**

| Category | Profit | Popularity | Action |
|----------|--------|------------|--------|
| **Stars** | High | High | Feature in prime spots, promote heavily |
| **Puzzles** | High | Low | Reposition, improve description, add visual appeal |
| **Plough Horses** | Low | High | Maintain, consider slight price increase |
| **Dogs** | Low | Low | Remove or re-engineer |

**Note:** High food cost items can still be Stars if priced appropriately. Focus on contribution margin, not just cost %.

**Deliverable:** Menu Engineering Matrix with food cost data

## Phase 2: Content Development (Day 2-3)

### 2.1 Item Selection
**Guidelines:**
- 7±2 items per category (cognitive load limit)
- Balance: 60% proven sellers, 40% innovation
- Include 2-3 signature items unique to restaurant
- Dietary accommodations: vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free options

### 2.2 Description Writing
**Formula:**
```
[Premium Ingredient] + [Preparation Method] + [Sensory Detail]
```

**Power Words:**
- Handcrafted, artisan, house-made, signature
- Fire-grilled, slow-roasted, pan-seared
- Fresh, seasonal, locally sourced

**Examples:**
- ❌ "Grilled chicken with vegetables"
- ✅ "Fire-grilled free-range chicken with seasonal roasted vegetables and herb compound butter"

### 2.3 Pricing Strategy
**Cost-First Pricing Formula:**
```
Menu Price = (Food Cost + Labor Cost) ÷ (1 − Target Margin %)
```

**Example:**
- Food cost: $4.50
- Labor cost: $2.00
- Target margin: 70%
- Menu price = ($4.50 + $2.00) ÷ 0.30 = **$21.67** → round to **$21.99**

**Psychological Techniques:**
1. **Anchoring**: Place highest-priced item first in section
2. **Charm Pricing**: $18.99 vs $19.00
3. **Remove Currency Symbols**: "18" vs "$18" (reduces price sensitivity)
4. **Decoy Item**: One expensive item makes others seem reasonable

**Pricing Guardrails:**
- Never price below food cost + minimum margin
- Know your break-even point per item
- Account for comp/voids (typically 2-5%)
- Review vendor costs monthly, adjust prices quarterly if needed

**Deliverable:** Menu Content Document with cost-based pricing rationale

## Phase 3: Design & Layout (Day 4-5)

### 3.1 Visual Hierarchy
**Sweet Spots** (place high-margin items here):
1. Top-right corner (prime real estate)
2. Center of menu
3. Top-left corner

### 3.2 Color Strategy
| Color | Effect | Use For |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Red/Orange** | Stimulates appetite | Headers, callouts, high-margin items |
| **Green** | Signals freshness | Healthy options, vegetarian items |
| **Black/White** | Elegance, sophistication | Fine dining, premium positioning |
| **Warm Tones** | Comfort, welcoming | Casual dining, comfort food |

### 3.3 Typography
- **Maximum 2-3 fonts** throughout
- **Hierarchy:**
  - Restaurant name: 24-36pt
  - Section headers: 18-24pt
  - Item names: 14-16pt bold
  - Descriptions: 11-12pt
  - Prices: 12-14pt (same or smaller than item name)

### 3.4 Layout Specifications
**Single Page:**
- 30-40% white space
- Clear section divisions
- Logical flow: Appetizers → Salads → Entrées → Desserts

**Multi-Page:**
- 2-panel preferred over booklet
- Entrées on right panel (highest visibility)
- Beverages on separate insert or back page

**Digital Menus:**
- Touch-friendly: minimum 44px tap targets
- Scrollable sections
- Quick-load images
- Easy price updates

**Deliverable:** Menu Design Mockup

## Phase 4: Review & Refinement (Day 6)

### 4.1 Quality Checklist
- [ ] All high-margin items in sweet spots
- [ ] Descriptions compelling and accurate
- [ ] Pricing consistent with strategy
- [ ] Typography readable, hierarchy clear
- [ ] Brand colors and fonts applied
- [ ] Dietary icons/labels included
- [ ] Seasonal items marked
- [ ] QR code for digital menu (if applicable)

### 4.2 Client Review
**Present:**
- Design mockup (print or digital)
- Pricing rationale
- Menu engineering recommendations
- Revision timeline

**Gather feedback on:**
- Visual appeal
- Item descriptions
- Price points
- Overall flow

## Performance Metrics

**Track for 30 days post-launch:**
| Metric | Target | Measurement |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Average check increase | +8-12% | POS data comparison |
| High-margin item sales | +15% | Sales by item report |
| Customer feedback score | 4.5/5 | Review sentiment analysis |
| Menu item turnover | <10% | Items ordered at least once |

## Best Practices

### Seasonal Updates
- Review menu quarterly
- Rotate 15-20% of items seasonally
- Update descriptions for seasonal ingredients
- Communicate changes via email/social

### Digital Integration
- Link QR codes to updatable digital menu
- Enable online ordering if applicable
- Include allergen filtering
- Add high-quality food photography

### Staff Training
- Memorize 3 key items per section
- Know ingredient sourcing stories
- Understand pricing rationale
- Practice upselling techniques
