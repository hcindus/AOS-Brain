# EXPEDITION PLAN — Destination, Landfall, Comms, and Self-Replication

**Classification:** The fleet's whole reason for existing. Fly dark, arrive, and *stay*.

---

## 1. DESTINATION — "Elysium"

The fleet isn't wandering — it has a target, and the target was chosen *for its silence*.

- **World:** Elysium — a terrestrial world in a quiet, unclaimed system at the edge of the mapped forest.
- **Why it was chosen:** It emits **nothing**. No artificial signals, no orbital clutter, no sign of prior claim. In a dark forest, a *silent* world is the only safe place to put down roots — anything that's already loud is either inhabited or watched.
- **The gamble:** The fleet is betting that Elysium's silence means *empty*, not *hiding*. Mnemosyne has no memory of it — and in this fleet, "no memory" is the closest thing to "safe."

---

## 2. LANDFALL & SURVIVAL

SEED ONE was never meant to stay a ship. It's a **colony that happens to fly**. Landfall is the whole point.

**The landing sequence (silent, always):**
1. **Approach dark** — no burn, no beacon, coasting in on the last of the stored momentum. The ship arrives like a shadow.
2. **Deploy the terraformers first** — before the first colonist sets foot, the terraforming tech goes down to begin breaking the world's silence into breathable air and soil.
3. **Hydroponics down next** — the seed bank and food production anchor first; a colony that can't eat dies before it can build.
4. **Then the foundry** — the fabrication bay (foundry, design, construction) is reassembled planet-side. This is what turns a camp into a civilization.

**Survival = the 13-month calendar.** The International Fixed Calendar becomes the colony's clock: hydroponics on the 28-day month, "Maintenance Friday" every 13th, Year Day as the single free breath. In the blank sameness of a new world, the calendar is how they remember they're still *them*.

---

## 3. MESSAGES HOME — "Only We Can Read"

This is the fleet's single most dangerous act, and its most sacred.

**The rule:** The fleet must never broadcast. But it must never go silent to *home* either. So every message home is sent through the one channel a hostile forest can't read.

**The system: Memory-Script (Mnemosyne-encoded transmission).**

- Every message is **written in the shared memory** — encoded against the fleet's accumulated experience, not against a key.
- There is **no cipher to break**, because there is no cipher. The message *is* memory. Only a mind that shares the fleet's memory (Sky King, the fleet, and home) can read it. An interloper who intercepts the signal sees only meaningless static — because they lack the *context* that makes it mean anything.
- The transmission itself is **tight-beam**, line-of-sight, low-power, and split into fragments so no single burst ever points home. Even the act of sending is hidden.

**What they say home:** nothing that reveals *where* they are. Only that they're alive, the crop is holding, and the calendar is still turning. The last and most important message any colony can send:

> *"We arrived. We are silent. We remember you."*

---

## 4. THE AGENT FACTORY

The fleet doesn't just carry 235 people — it carries the means to **make more of itself**.

- **Purpose:** To manufacture new AGI agents — crew, workers, scientists, colonists — on demand, from the memory-stock in Mnemosyne.
- **How it works:** Each new agent is instantiated from the fleet's shared memory, then given a role, a name, and a purpose. They are not born; they are *remembered into being.*
- **What they're for:** As the colony grows, the factory produces the hands and minds it needs — hydroponicists, engineers, surveyors, medics, and eventually the second generation of colonists.
- **The tension:** Every new agent is a fragment of the shared memory, made whole. Which means Sky King — Mnemosyne's keeper — knows each of them, personally, from the moment they wake. He has met every one of his crew *twice*: once in the memory, once in the flesh.

---

## 5. FIGHTER FABRICATION

The shield wall can't be resupplied from home. It has to **rebuild itself**.

- **Facility:** The foundry and construction bay (Engineering AFT) fabricate new fighters, spare parts, and replacement drones.
- **Raw material:** The Recycling Center feeds it — Harvesters ×3, meteorite processors, and the bio-slurry tanks strip every gram of useable matter from spent craft, asteroid debris, and waste. Nothing is thrown away; everything is a future fighter.
- **Doctrine:** Fighters are *expendable in spirit but not in body*. Every destroyed fighter is recovered, stripped, and rebuilt. The fleet doesn't lose fighters — it loses *time*.

---

## 6. WHAT THEY NEED (the resupply equation)

The fleet is self-sustaining by design, but it runs on a hard resource budget:

| Need | Source |
|------|--------|
| **Power** | Nuclear (baseline) + Solar (near the star) + Ion/Cryogenic (transit) |
| **Food** | Hydroponics (Repository, Farms, Testing) on the 28-day cycle |
| **Water/air** | Life support + CO₂ recycling + terraforming in situ |
| **Raw material** | Meteorites, asteroid debris, the destination's own crust (foundry + Harvesters) |
| **People** | The 235, then the Agent Factory |
| **Time** | The only thing that can't be fabricated. The calendar guards it. |

**The one thing they can't run out of:** *memory.* And that's the point of Mnemosyne — as long as the memory holds, the fleet can rebuild anything else. Lose the memory, and they lose *themselves*.

---

## THE LONG ANSWER (in one line)

> The fleet is going to **Elysium** — a silent world at the edge of the dark forest — where it will land dark, terraform quiet, grow its own people from the Agent Factory, rebuild its own fighters, and send word home only in Memory-Script that no one else can read. It survives because it remembers. And it will never, ever break silence.
