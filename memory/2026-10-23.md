**Insight anchor (from Asimov-1 video Captain shared):** "What keeps a robot upright isn't stiffness — it's where the ground pushes back (CoP inside the foot). Keep the torso upright on purpose." This is why naive `hold` PD (29% alive) can never graduate; the controller must manage CoM/CoP, not just hold joint angles.

## Controller built — progress + honest finding (21:20 UTC)
Built `controllers/balance.py` + `ControllerPilot` wrapper (controller reads full MuJoCo state via `attach(env)`; pilots stay obs-based). Wired `balance` into roster/eval/graduate.

- **Fixed a real bug:** `hip_pitch` (and `knee`) are mirrored across the legs (L +y / R -y axis); same-sign torque was anti-symmetric and twisted the robot (7.7% alive). Mirrored the sign → fixed.
- **Result:** `balance` (35% alive) beats `hold` (29%). Graduates at 0.35 → still `BACK_TO_TRAINING`.
- **Honest finding (structural, not tuning):** a pure feedback controller (PD hold + torso-upright feedback) ceilings at ~35% upright no matter the gains. The video's thesis holds: you need the **ZMP/CoP planner** layer (footstep + CoM trajectory so CoP stays inside the foot), not just feedback. The RP1's feet are 4 tiny contact points with ±5.7° ankle roll — CoP management is everything.
- **Next layer:** write the ZMP-preview planner (linear inverted pendulum + whole-body QP), or go the RL path via the GPU dispatch socket. Both are the "real muscle"; feedback alone can't graduate a walker.
