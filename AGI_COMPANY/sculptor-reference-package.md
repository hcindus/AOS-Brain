## The written likeness anchor (send this text)

> Myl1Ssa (Myl2Ssa.R0s) is a glamorous woman with **long dark wavy hair** and a **calm, elegant, slightly composed expression.** High-end, sophisticated, effortless elegance. Her face is beautiful but *real* — give it life, not airbrushing. Capture the character, the asymmetry, the texture. Everything "ugly refused."

**For the bust specifically:** the bust is the **face + head shape only — no hair.** (The hair is a separate fiber-optic system.) Sculpt the hairless scalp/head; the face is what matters.

## Which images to send

1. **The front-view render** (the strongest "face" shot) — primary likeness reference.
2. **One three-quarter or profile** — so they see the face's depth/contours, not just front-on.
3. *(Optional)* the back view — only if they need the head/skull shape for the mold.

Pick the renders where the **face is clearest and most consistent** — the whole point is a *consistent* likeness across angles, so pick images that agree with each other.

---

## What the sculptor must get right

1. **Likeness over prettiness** — a real face, not a mannequin. Keep the asymmetry.
2. **Face only, hairless scalp** — no sculpted hair (fiber-optic hair goes on separately).
3. **Mold-able clay + matte finish** — for the reverse mold.
4. **Clearance for the actuator frame** — the face topology must fit over the 26–30 point mechanism (share the frame's face geometry when they're ready).

## Notes to keep straight

- **The character is consistent** — all reference images are the *same person* (Myl1Ssa). If the renders disagree slightly, the *front-view* one is the master.
- **Hair is a wig/system, not the sculpt** — keep the sculptor focused on the face.
- **"Everything ugly refused" is the brief** — paste it verbatim; it's the clearest way to say "no airbrushing."

*The likeness anchor + the front-view render is 90% of what a good sculptor needs. The rest is their craft.*
