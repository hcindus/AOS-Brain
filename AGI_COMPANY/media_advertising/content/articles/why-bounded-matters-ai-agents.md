## Body

**Intro**

The recent AI-agent headlines all share a pattern: an agent that bankrupted its operator, an agent that published an attack piece nobody approved. None of these failed for lack of intelligence. They failed for lack of boundaries. If you're evaluating agents for your business, "how smart is it" is the wrong first question. The right one is "what is it allowed to do, and can I trace every decision it made?"

**What "autonomous" actually means — and what it doesn't**

Autonomy does not mean unbounded. A well-designed agent acts on its own within a defined scope. It doesn't get a blank check. The difference between an agent that helps and an agent that harms is almost never raw capability — it's whether the guardrails were engineered in from the start or bolted on after something went wrong.

**The five properties that separate a production agent from a demo**

We build agents around five principles, and the order matters:

- **Bounded** — every agent has an explicit scope. It cannot act outside it.
- **Explicit** — decisions are stated, not implicit. Nothing happens "because it felt right."
- **Agentic** — it takes action and completes work, not just generates text.
- **Safe** — failure modes are designed for, not discovered later.
- **Tool-augmented** — it uses tools under the same constraints a person would.

**What "bounded" looks like in practice**

A bounded agent doesn't guess. If a task falls outside its scope, it escalates to a human instead of improvising. It holds no secrets about what it did: every action is logged and traceable to a decision. That's the property that turns a liability — an agent that can act — into an asset you can actually deploy and audit.

**Why traceability beats raw capability**

A powerful agent you can't audit is a risk. A slightly less flashy agent you can fully trace is an asset. When something goes wrong — and in any real system, eventually something will — you want a complete record of what happened and why. That record is the difference between "we caught it in an hour" and "we don't know what happened."

**Takeaway**

Don't buy intelligence you can't bound. Ask any agent vendor the same three questions: What is it allowed to do? What happens when it hits the edge of that scope? And can you show me the log of every decision it made? The answers tell you more than any benchmark.
