## Body

**Intro**

Every point-of-sale counter runs on a small set of consumables and hardware — receipt paper, ink/ribbons, cash drawers, and (for many) a scale. But "what do I actually need?" is a question vendors rarely answer straight. Here's the honest breakdown, by business type, so you buy the right thing the first time.

**The three consumables every register needs**

1. **Receipt paper** — thermal (most modern registers) or bond (impact/ribbon printers). Get the *width* right: 2¼" (57mm) or 3⅛" (80mm). Using the wrong width jams the printer.
2. **Ink / ribbons** — only for impact printers. Thermal printers need no ink. Don't buy ribbons for a thermal printer.
3. **Cash drawer** — driven by the register or receipt printer. Match the interface (RJ12/RJ11) and the bill/coin layout (4B5C vs 4B8C).

**By business type**

- **Quick-service / café:** thermal printer (58mm), 2¼" paper, small footprint drawer. You rarely need a scale.
- **Bar / restaurant:** thermal printer + spill-resistant drawer + (optionally) a portion-control scale for consistent pours.
- **Retail / grocery:** thermal printer (80mm) + price-computing or label-printing scale + a standard drawer.
- **Deli / specialty food:** label-printing scale (like the CAS CL-series) + die-cut labels + 3⅛" paper.

**What you *don't* need**

- Ribbons, if your printer is thermal.
- A second cash drawer "just in case" — one per register is standard.
- Premium paper for internal/kitchen tickets — standard thermal is fine.

**The one rule**

Match every consumable to the *machine*, not the brand. Paper width, ribbon type, and drawer interface are the only three specs that matter for the basics. Everything else is preference.
