## Underground / Earthbag / Cob Building Methods

## I. EARTHBAG CONSTRUCTION

### Overview
- **Origin:** 100 years of military sandbag structures
- **Modern use:** Homes, offices, schools, orphanages
- **Properties:** Sustainable, nontoxic, quiet, rodent-proof, fire/hurricane/flood resistant
- **Also:** Bullet and blast resistant

### Materials Required
| Item | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Shovel | Digging, soil work |
| Bucket | Filling earthbags |
| Garden hose | Water for mix |
| Wheelbarrow | Transport material |
| Tamper | Compacting bags |
| Metal slider | Placing bags |
| Earthbags | Sandbags 18"x30" |
| Barbed wire | Tensile strength |
| Level | Ensuring even courses |

### Construction Steps

**1. Site Preparation**
- Clear and level building area
- Save topsoil for landscaping
- Stake out project
- Bury plumbing/electric safely

**2. Foundation**
- Rubble trench: 18"-24" deep, wider than walls
- Fill with gravel
- Leave 6"-12" for earthbag courses

**3. Gravel Courses**
- Fill bags 3/4 full with gravel
- Tuck end underneath
- Tamp after each course
- Purpose: moisture barrier

**4. Barbed Wire**
- 4-point between each course
- 1 strand for vertical walls
- 2 strands for domes/vaults
- Extend 18" and connect with tie wire

**5. Wall Construction**
- Use metal slider for placement
- Fill with soil (most climates) or insulation (extreme climates)
- Tamp solid after each course
- Keep walls plumb, straight, level

**6. Openings**
- Door forms: 6" above grade
- Window forms at appropriate levels
- Can use wood forms, barrels, tires, wagon wheels, steel culvert

**7. Bond Beam**
- Top of vertical walls
- Reinforced concrete or wood
- Supports roof from snow/wind loads

**8. Roof**
- Wide overhangs: 36" (1 meter) to protect walls

**9. Plaster**
- Earth plaster (protected by overhangs)
- Lime plaster for domes (more exposed)
- Living roof option

### Soil Requirements
- **Ideal mix:** 25% clay, 75% sandy soil
- Most subsoils work
- Can add sand or clay to adjust
- Road base, reject fines, adobe soil, fill dirt all work

### Cost Estimates
- Very low material cost
- Labor-intensive but achievable
- ~$50-100/sq ft (owner-built)

## II. UNDERGROUND / HOBBIT HOUSES

### Key Concept
- Earth-sheltered design
- Natural insulation from soil
- Passive solar gain
- Minimal environmental impact

### Examples (from Country Plans)

**Montana Underground House Features:**
- **Walls:** Stick frame with reject 2x4s, covered with salvaged plywood
- **Interior wall finish:** Corrugated sheeting from old gold mine mill
- **Floor options:**
  - Cob floor + boiled linseed oil (durable)
  - Soil cement (needs regular sealing)
  - Paper bag floor ( polyurethane sealed)
  - **CBRI light duty concrete** (recommended: thin, colorful, durable)

### CBRI Floor System (India)
- 1/8 thickness of standard slab
- Colorful, environmentally friendly
- As durable as full slab
- Uses plunger support piers (soft ground)
- Rated 50 psf, tested to 250 psf

### Ventilation
- Critical for underground spaces
- Cross ventilation via windows
- Solar array for off-grid power
- Wind generator backup

### Cost Effective Features
- Reclaimed materials
- Owner-built labor
- Minimal code requirements
- Evolving design (no rigid plans needed)

## III. COB CONSTRUCTION

### What is Cob?
- Layered mud (sand + clay + straw)
- Sculptural, organic walls
- Thermal mass properties
- Fire resistant

### Benefits
- Sustainable (earth + straw)
- Nontoxic
- Excellent thermal mass
- Beautiful organic forms
- Low cost

## IV. MODOC COUNTY APPLICATION

### Why Earth Homes Work Here

| Factor | Benefit |
|--------|---------|
| **Cold winters** | Earth provides natural insulation (R-1 per inch) |
| **12" rain/year** | Good drainage needed, but earth walls breathe |
| **High elevation** | Solar gain maximized with underground |
| **Off-grid goal** | Low material cost, owner-built |
| **Remote location** | Minimal code, affordable materials |

### Potential Structures at Hudn Hall

| Structure | Type | Purpose |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **Root cellar** | Earthbag | Food storage, cool temperature |
| **Guest cottage** | Cob/earthbag | Secondary dwelling |
| **Garden walls** | Cob | Terracing, raised beds |
| **Workshop** | Earthbag | Tool storage, workspace |
| **Water shed** | Earthbag | Rainwater infrastructure |

### Design Recommendations
1. **South-facing openings** for solar gain
2. **Wide roof overhangs** (36"+)
3. **Rubble trench foundation** (frost protection)
4. **Gravel course at grade** (moisture protection)
5. **Living roof** for insulation + aesthetics
