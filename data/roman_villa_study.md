## Source: Vitruvius — De Architectura, Book VI

## I. VITRUVIUS ON PRIVATE HOUSES (Book VI)

### Core Principle: Climate-Based Design
> "The method of building suited to Egypt would be very improper in Spain, and that in use in Pontus would be absurd at Rome."

**Key Insight:** Architecture must respond to:
- **Sun's course** (orientation)
- **Climate zone** (north = cold, south = hot)
- **Local materials** (stone, brick, wood)

---

## II. ROMAN VILLA ELEMENTS

### The Atrium (Central Court)
The heart of the Roman house — open central court for light and ventilation.

| Element | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **Atrium** | Central open court, collects rainwater |
| **Compluvium** | Opening in roof to admit light/rain |
| **Impluvium** | Central basin catching rainwater |
| **Alae** | Wing rooms on either side |
| **Tablinum** | Master room/office opening to atrium |

**Proportions (Vitruvius):**
- Atrium width = 3/5 of length (standard ratio)
- Height to beams = 3/4 of length
- Alae width scales with atrium size

### The Peristyle (Garden Court)
- **Columns** surrounding garden courtyard
- **Open-air passage** between house and garden
- **Greek influence** — outdoor living space
- Intercolumniations: 3-4 column diameters

### Room Proportions
| Room Type | Ratio |
|-----------|-------|
| **Triclinium** (dining) | Length = 2 × Width |
| **Exedrae** (hallways) | Height = 1.5 × Width |
| **General oblong rooms** | Height = (L + W) / 2 |

## III. CLIMATE ADAPTATION (Vitruvius, Ch. 1)

### Northern Climates (Cold)
- **Buildings:** Arched, enclosed, protected
- **Orientation:** Face warm southern aspects
- **Purpose:** Capture and retain heat

### Southern Climates (Hot)
- **Buildings:** Exposed, open to air flow
- **Orientation:** Face north and east
- **Purpose:** Avoid heat, maximize ventilation

### Temperate (Italy)
- **Principle:** Balance between extremes
- **Result:** "Intermediate" design responsive to conditions

## IV. APPLICATION TO HUDN HALL

### High Desert (Modoc County, CA)

**Climate:** Cold winters, warm summers, 12" rain/year, high elevation

**Roman Villa Principles Adapted:**

#### 1. Orientation
- **South-facing** for winter solar gain (cold winters)
- **Wide overhangs** for summer shade (warm summers)
- **East-facing** morning sun, avoid western heat

#### 2. The Atrium Concept → Great Room
- Central gathering space with **clarestory windows**
- **Rainwater collection** (compluvium-inspired) feeding Water Shed
- Open plan connecting to **peristyle** (covered patio)

#### 3. The Peristyle → Covered Patio + Garden
- Columns supporting **ramada/covered walk**
- Indoor-outdoor living (Roman: "outdoor rooms")
- Connects to **kitchen garden** and **orchard**

#### 4. Room Relationships
| Roman Element | Hudn Hall Adaptation |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Atrium | Great room with clerestory |
| Tablinum | Master suite / office |
| Alae | Guest rooms / storage |
| Peristyle | Covered patio + garden court |
| Triclinium | Outdoor dining + indoor dining |
| Bath (if used) | Sauna / hot room |

#### 5. Thermal Mass
- **Stone fireplace** (rumford-style) = Roman hypocaust concept
- **Stone floors** = thermal mass for heat retention
- **Thermal windows** = glass "walls" for solar gain
